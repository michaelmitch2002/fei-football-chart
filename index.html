How does committee rank?

The 2026 college football season has made me think a lot about rankings and metrics. For the past three weeks, I have watched on Tuesday evening as the playoff committee unveils a ranking of teams that manages to upset every fanbase except one (Alabama). It does not especially help the committee’s case in the eyes of the public that they use a proprietary and undisclosed set of metrics for this ranking.

We are given an output we do not understand with little insight into the inputs.

My goal for this project is to try and generate a little intuition about the ranking process. People talk about rankings as a tug-of-war between “best” vs. “most deserving”. I disagree with the language used. I prefer “how you play” and “who you play”. “How you play” is judged by a power rating, and “who you play” is judged by strength of record (SOR).

This page presents both sets of ratings on their own for weeks 11 and 12 of the 2026 college football season, as well as a weighted sum of the two. I also attempt to solve a regression problem for the appropriate weighting between “how you play” and “who you play”. Finally, there is a “head-to-head criteria” option that works from the first-place team to the last-place team in a single pass and swaps two teams if the higher-ranked team lost to the team directly below it.

I hope this provides you with some insight into the rankings!

Metric overview

Let me describe these metrics quickly. I use Brian Fremeau’s FEI metrics for this project. His power ratings are the FEI ratings. They measure opponent-adjusted possession efficiency: the expected scoring advantage per non-garbage possession on a neutral field against an average opponent. Strength of schedule (SOS) is the number of losses a benchmark team would expect to have against a schedule. Obviously, this number of expected losses is a function of the quality of the benchmark team. SOR is simply the difference between the expected number of losses and the actual number of losses.[1]

[1] Brian Fremeau, 2026 FEI Ratings, BCF Toys. See the definitions of FEI, SOS, and SOR above the ratings table.
